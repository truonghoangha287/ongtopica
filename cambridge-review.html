Ongtopica English — Material Review against the Cambridge Programme

A pedagogical review of the current vocabulary app measured against the Cambridge English Qualifications for Young Learners (Pre A1 Starters, A1 Movers, A2 Flyers) — where it is strong, what to improve level by level, and which exercise types are missing (including the two you photographed).

14 word sets · 232 words 5 activity types Word-level only Reviewed 5 Jul 2026

The one-paragraph verdict

Ongtopica is an excellent single-word vocabulary trainer — audio-first, picture-supported, offline, with a clean four-stage mastery ladder. But Cambridge YLE, even at Starters level, tests language inside sentences and pictures-in-context, not just isolated words. Right now the app exercises only the lowest rung: recognising and spelling one word at a time. The two exercises you photographed — a word-bank cloze and a picture-scene question task — are exactly the two rungs above it that are missing.

B
Strong foundation, narrow scope
Covers ~2 of the 9 Starters skill areas well; 0% of Movers.

✅ What is working well

  • Audio-first design — every word has native audio; matches how young learners should meet vocabulary.
  • Four-stage progressive mastery (introduce → recognise → unscramble → fill-blank) is genuine spaced, staged learning.
  • Spelling activities already mirror Starters Reading & Writing Part 3.
  • Listening-match mirrors the Listening paper's matching tasks.
  • Picture-supported, offline PWA, per-child progress — great for the 5–8 age group.

⚠️ The core gaps

  • No sentence-level language. Every task is one word in isolation.
  • No reading of connected text (cloze, riddles, short stories).
  • No picture-scene comprehension — the "What are they doing?" task type.
  • No grammar-in-context (plurals, prepositions, present continuous, have got, can).
  • No speaking / pronunciation practice at all.
  • No Movers content — vocabulary, tenses, or longer texts.
Headline recommendation: keep the vocabulary engine as the "warm-up," but add a second layer of sentence- and picture-level exercises so a child moves from knowing the word "ball" to reading "the man has a ball on his head" and answering a question about it. That is the leap from a flashcard app to a Cambridge-aligned course.

Improving the Starters level

You already own the vocabulary layer. These additions turn it into a Starters-complete course. Ordered by impact. Click each to expand.

Your current fill-in-blank fills a single letter ("be_r"). The Cambridge task fills a whole word into a sentence, chosen from a picture word-bank. This is the single biggest missing rung.

  • Show a 3–5 sentence text (a riddle or description) with 5 gaps.
  • Give a picture word-bank (tree, bag, eyes, fruit, kitchen…) — reuse existing word images.
  • Child taps a picture-word to drop it into the gap.

Reuses: your 232 word images + audio, the celebration/mascot system, the retry logic.

Show one scene picture; ask 3–5 WH-questions with 1-word answers: "Where are they?" → "in a plane." Trains reading a question + reading a picture together — completely absent today.

Grammar it quietly teaches: prepositions (in/on/under), present continuous (What are they doing?), have got.

Show a picture + a sentence ("This is a cat.") — child taps ✓ or ✗. The gentlest possible entry into reading whole sentences. Easy to generate from your existing words + a true/false flag.

Starters is graded on specific structures. Add tiny drills:

  • Plurals: one cat / three cats — tap the right form.
  • Prepositions: "The ball is ___ the box" (in / on / under) with a picture.
  • this / that / these / those with near/far pictures.
  • Present continuous: "He is ___" (running / eating) with an action image.

Audio says "colour the small dog"/"put the ball under the table" → child taps/colours. Trains listening for detail + prepositions, not just single-word recognition.

Even a simple record-and-play-back ("Say: elephant") or "point to the…" tap game covers the Speaking paper, which is currently 0%. No scoring needed — self-comparison is enough at this age.

Reaching the Movers level

Movers is currently at 0%. It is not "more of the same" — it is a step up in text length, grammar, and thinking. Here is what a Movers track needs.

📚 Content you must add

  • ~450 more words to reach the ~900 cumulative Movers list — jobs, weather, adverbs, feelings, more actions.
  • Past-tense verbs (went, saw, ate) — a whole word class the Starters data doesn't have.
  • Adjectives for comparison (big / bigger / biggest).
  • Short stories & paragraphs — Movers reads connected text, not single sentences.

🧩 Exercise types Movers needs

  • Definition matching (R&W 1): "You wear these on your feet." → shoes.
  • Story gap-fill from a longer text (R&W 3–4).
  • Picture-story sequencing + write 1–5 word answers (R&W 6).
  • Spot-the-difference between two pictures (Speaking Part 1).
  • Multiple-choice dialogue: pick the right reply.

Data-model additions

Your Word objects are word-only today. Add sibling content collections rather than overloading Word:

// new: a short readable text with word-bank gaps  (Part 4)
interface ClozeText {
  id: string; wordSetId: string; level: 'starters' | 'movers';
  sentences: string;           // "I come from a {gap1}. I am {gap2}."
  gaps: { id: string; answerWordId: string }[];
  bankWordIds: string[];       // pictures reused from your 232 words
}

// new: a picture scene with WH-questions           (Part 5)
interface PictureScene {
  id: string; imageAsset: string; level: 'starters' | 'movers';
  questions: { q: string; answer: string; choices?: string[] }[];
}

// small add to Word — powers sentence-level activities
interface Word { /* …existing… */
  exampleSentence?: string;    // "The cat is on the box."
  partOfSpeech?: 'noun'|'verb'|'adjective';
  plural?: string;             // "cats"
}

  2. Add picture-qaDelivers Photo 2. Start with 1-word multiple-choice answers, then free-type.
  3. Add yes-no-sentence + preposition-pickCheap grammar-in-context wins; completes the Starters R&W paper.

Bottom line: Ongtopica is a great word app. Steps 1–3 above — mostly data plus two new screens — would make it a genuine Cambridge Starters practice tool, and steps 4–5 extend it to Speaking and Movers.
